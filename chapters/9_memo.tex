\textbf{To: Show Management}\\
\textbf{Subject: Recommendation for a More Stable and Transparent Voting System}

Our analyses indicate that controversy risk concentrates in weeks where judges' evaluations and audience preferences diverge.
To preserve audience agency while preventing extreme popularity from overwhelming technical performance, we recommend piloting
\emph{Judge Safeguard + Smoothed Rank (JS-SR)}.

Under JS-SR, each week designates a \emph{Safeguard Zone}: contestants above a judges-score percentile threshold (top $\tau$)
are immune from elimination. The remaining contestants compete in a ``battleground'' where elimination is determined by a
transparent weighted blend of judges' share and fan share:
\[
S^{(JS)}_{i,t}=w_J\, q^J_{i,t} + (1-w_J)\, p_{i,t}.
\]
Management controls two intuitive knobs: $\tau$ (how many top technical performers are protected) and $w_J$ (judge--fan balance within
the battleground). We recommend selecting $(\tau,w_J)$ from a robust region identified by historical replays and uncertainty stress tests,
then deploying JS-SR in a one-season ``shadow mode'' computed in parallel with the broadcast rule to quantify differences in eliminations,
finalists, and winner outcomes before adoption.

Operationally, we recommend a one-season \emph{shadow deployment} in which the proposed rule is computed in parallel with the broadcast rule
to quantify differences in eliminations, finalists, and winner outcomes, accompanied by a clear public explanation of tie-handling and the role
of any judges' save mechanism. This provides a controlled, auditable path to improving fairness perception and reducing outcome volatility
without sacrificing interpretability.
