\subsection{Outcome definitions}
\label{subsec:outcome_definitions_d1}

To disentangle how outcomes are shaped by professional partners versus celebrity attributes, we model
judges' evaluations and inferred fan preferences as two distinct but related outcomes.
For comparability across seasons and weeks, we work with \emph{shares} rather than raw scores.
Let $A_{s,t}$ denote the active set in season $s$, week $t$.

\paragraph{Judges' outcome.}
Define the judges' score share
\begin{equation}
q^{J}_{i,t}=\frac{J_{i,t}}{\sum_{r\in A_{s,t}}J_{r,t}},
\end{equation}
and use a logit transform with a small $\epsilon$ to avoid boundary issues:
\begin{equation}
y^{J}_{i,t}=\log\frac{q^{J}_{i,t}+\epsilon}{1-q^{J}_{i,t}+\epsilon}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Fans' outcome.}
Let Task~A provide the inferred vote share $p_{i,t}$ (denoted $q^{V}_{i,t}$). We similarly define
\begin{equation}
y^{V}_{i,t}=\log\frac{p_{i,t}+\epsilon}{1-p_{i,t}+\epsilon}.
\end{equation}
We adopt share + logit for cross-season comparability and interpret coefficients on the log-odds scale.

\subsection{Modeling results: separating ``who judges reward'' vs.\ ``who fans support''}
\label{subsec:modeling_results_d1}

We fit two mixed-effects models on the season--week panel to attribute variation to
(i) celebrity covariates and (ii) professional dancer partners.\cite{law2015simulation}

\paragraph{Model 1: Judge Model.}
\begin{equation}
y^{J}_{i,t}=\beta_{0}+\beta_X^{\top}X_{i}+\beta_T f(t)+FE_s+u_i+v_{d(i)}+\epsilon_{i,t},
\end{equation}
where $X_i$ collects time-invariant celebrity attributes (e.g., age, occupation indicators),
$f(t)$ captures week trend, $FE_s$ is a season fixed effect, $u_i\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma_u^2)$ is a celebrity random effect,
and $v_{d(i)}\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma_v^2)$ is a professional-dancer random effect.
A key hypothesis test is $H_0:\sigma_v^2=0$ (no pro-dancer effect on judges). %:contentReference[oaicite:13]{index=13}

\paragraph{Model 2: Vote Model.}
To model inferred fan support while allowing mediation through judges' performance, we include $y^{J}_{i,t}$:
\begin{equation}
y^{V}_{i,t}=\gamma_{0}+\gamma_J y^{J}_{i,t}+\gamma_X^{\top}X_{i}+\gamma_T f(t)+FE_s+a_i+b_{d(i)}+\eta_{i,t},
\end{equation}
with $a_i\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma_a^2)$ and $b_{d(i)}\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma_b^2)$ capturing celebrity and pro-dancer random effects
on fan preferences, respectively. The coefficient $\gamma_J$ measures the transmission strength from judges to fans. %:contentReference[oaicite:14]{index=14}

\paragraph{Comparing judges vs.\ fans.}
For each shared attribute $k$, define the preference gap
\begin{equation}
\Delta_k=\gamma_k-\beta_k.
\end{equation}
$\Delta_k>0$ indicates fans prefer the attribute more than judges do; $\Delta_k<0$ indicates the opposite.
We report $\Delta_k$ with uncertainty bands (via bootstrap) whenever vote inference uncertainty is non-negligible. %:contentReference[oaicite:15]{index=15}

\paragraph{Quantifying professional-dancer impact (magnitude, not just significance).}
We quantify the fraction of outcome variance attributable to professional dancers using ICC-style ratios:
\begin{equation}
ICC^{J}_{pro}=\frac{\sigma_v^{2}}{\sigma_v^{2}+\sigma_u^{2}+\sigma^{2}},\qquad
ICC^{V}_{pro}=\frac{\sigma_b^{2}}{\sigma_b^{2}+\sigma_a^{2}+\sigma_\eta^{2}}.
\end{equation}
We treat BLUP rankings as descriptive summaries of the fitted random effects rather than causal estimates to compare whether the
same pros are ``good for judges'' and ``good for fans.''
%:contentReference[oaicite:16]{index=16}

\paragraph{Uncertainty propagation.}
Because fan votes are inferred, we propagate uncertainty either by (i) bootstrap re-estimation of votes and refitting the Vote Model, or (ii) weighted regression using the interval width from Task A as an uncertainty proxy; we report confidence intervals accordingly.
%:contentReference[oaicite:17]{index=17}
